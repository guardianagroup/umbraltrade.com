      How we work

      Umbral plots the 1-day and 4-hour support and resistance zones and generates these entries: in the direction of the trend on every market, on the 30-minute to 4-hour charts; entries outside the Bollinger band, against or with the move depending on the 1-hour, 4-hour and 1-day trend, on the 1 to 15-minute charts; the oversold buy on the US indices; and, on EUR/USD, USD/CAD and EUR/GBP, zone entries as a binary option, the first time price touches the midpoint of a zone. The video shows that zone entry step by step, with real candles.

      How the algorithm works

      1-day and 4-hour zones
      It draws the support and resistance zones of the higher timeframes and keeps them valid until a candle of their timeframe closes on the other side. It also marks institutional zones separately: the origin of the impulses that broke the structure.
      First touch, with price overextended
      It records every touch of every zone, and the zone entry only fires on the first one, when price arrives overextended. If it arrives with an impulse candle against it, it waits.
      A methodology for each asset class
      It does not work the same way on forex as on gold, crypto or indices: each asset class has its own management and scaling-in rules, and for each entry the algorithm chooses the product: a CFD with a trailing stop, a binary option or both.
      Real time, no repainting
      It analyses every new price and fixes each signal on the candle where it is generated. Failed entries stay visible: the chart shows the real history.

      Umbral is an analysis tool: it does not execute trades and is not financial advice.

      Three entry zones, never more than three

      Price rarely turns exactly at the support or resistance: it often breaks it to collect the liquidity behind it and then comes back. That is why the three entry zones sit on the other side, below the support and above the resistance, with a point where the plan stops being valid. Supports are green and resistances red.

      At a support, price comes down

      We mark price turns on 30 minutes, 1 hour, 4 hours and 1 day. A 1-day turn weighs three times a 1-hour one, 30-minute turns weigh little, and supports and resistances that price has already crossed twice are dropped. Few are left, and they are the ones that matter. While price sits on the support, you wait.

      Zone 1

      Price breaks the support. The first zone starts just below it, and that is the alert you get. From here you wait for price to make a floor inside the zones. One entry per zone: three at most in each break.

      Zone 2

      Price often keeps falling to take the liquidity behind it. That is where the second entry goes, again with its floor confirmed. The distance comes from that asset's real sweeps.

      Zone 3

      If the sweep runs deep there is a third entry, with a better average price. There is no fourth.

      Stop loss

      If price gets here, the support has truly broken and the plan is cancelled. You know it before you enter.

      At a resistance, upwards

      Same plan, upside down. Price breaks the resistance upwards: zone 1 starts just above it, zones 2 and 3 higher up, and the stop at the very top. You wait for price to make a ceiling inside the zones. The number always follows the order in which price arrives.

      Questions

      Where do the prices come from?

      In real time. Synthetics, forex, metals, indices, Bitcoin and Ethereum come from Deriv's public server: the same prices you see on its platform. The other cryptocurrencies come from Binance, and oil and natural gas from Hyperliquid, a contracts platform that tracks their price.

      Can I practise without risking money?

      Yes. Next to each asset's chart there is a panel to open binaries (Up or Down) and CFDs (Buy or Sell, with the leverage you choose) with €10,000 of virtual money. You see them drawn on the chart with their time left, and the Practice page keeps your history and your hit rate.

      Does it work for binaries and CFDs?

      Yes. The alert gives you the direction and the three entry prices. You choose the instrument, the size and the expiry.

      Do supports work on synthetic indices?

      Synthetics are generated by an audited random algorithm and do not react to news. The radar marks where price turned and which supports and resistances it is at now. No support guarantees a bounce.

      Who can trade binary options?

      It depends on the country and on the client category. In the European Union and the United Kingdom their sale to retail clients has been prohibited since 2018 and 2019; professional clients may trade them with authorised firms. In the United States they are only traded on CFTC-regulated exchanges. Umbral Trade is an analysis tool and does not sell these products: always check what you are allowed to trade in your country.

      Is this financial advice?

      No. Umbral Trade is an analysis tool. It does not manage money or guarantee results. Trading with leverage can make you lose everything you invested.

      1. What trading is

      Trading means opening positions to profit from price moves: buying if you think it will go up and selling if you think it will go down. You do not need to own the asset: with binaries and CFDs you only bet on the direction of the price.

      Nobody is always right. The difference between those who last and those who blow up their account is entering at good places and losing little when wrong.

      2. The markets you will see here

      Forex
      Currency pairs, like EUR/USD: how many dollars one euro is worth. Open Monday to Friday.
      Commodities
      Gold, silver, platinum, palladium, WTI oil, Brent oil and natural gas.
      Indices
      A basket of a country's large companies, like the US 500 or Germany 40.
      Crypto
      Bitcoin, Ethereum and other cryptocurrencies. They move a lot and never close.
      Synthetics
      Deriv indices that imitate a market. They trade 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and news does not affect them. Volatility 75 moves more than Volatility 10.

      3. Candles: how to read the chart

      Each candle sums up what price did over a period: where it opened, where it closed, and the highest and lowest it reached. Green: it closed higher than it opened, so it rose. Red: it closed lower, so it fell.

      The body runs from the open to the close. The wicks are where price reached and then gave back. A long lower wick at a support says buyers defended it.

      Heikin Ashi is another way to draw candles. Each one averages its price with the previous one, so they come out smoother: rises chain green candles and falls chain red ones, and the trend shows at a glance. Turn it on with the Heikin Ashi button on the chart bar (HA on small screens). Because they are an average, they do not show the exact price: when you trade, go by the current price label, which always shows the real price.

      4. Timeframes

      It is how long each candle lasts: 30 minutes (30m), 1 hour (1H), 4 hours (4H) or 1 day (1D). It is the same price seen with more or less zoom. The radar only uses these four: on timeframes of a few minutes, the commission weighs much more on each trade. The 1, 3, 5 and 15-minute charts have their own entries, outside the Bollinger band.

      A useful rule: find the level on a high timeframe (30m, 1H, 4H or 1D) and fine-tune the entry on a low one (1m, 3m, 5m or 15m). That is why the radar watches 30-minute, 1-hour, 4-hour and 1-day supports and resistances, and you can open up to four charts at once.

      5. Support and resistance

      A support is an area below the price where buyers showed up before and price bounced up. A resistance is an area above where sellers showed up and price fell.

      In Umbral a support is always green and below, and a resistance is always red and above. They are bands, not lines: price rarely turns at the exact cent. The higher the timeframe and the more times price has turned there, the stronger the zone.

      If price breaks a support and really moves away, that level can start acting as resistance. Umbral only changes its colour once price has moved far enough away.

      6. The three entry zones

      Price almost never turns right at the edge: it often goes a little further, sweeps the stops of those who entered early and then turns. That is why Umbral spreads the entry over three consecutive bands. Their width is not fixed: it comes from what price has done on that asset, so a jumpy asset has wider zones and a calm one narrower zones.

      • Zone 1: just past the broken support or resistance, as far as price usually goes before turning. It is the strongest colour.
      • Zone 2: the sweep, where liquidity usually gets triggered, out to the deepest sweeps.
      • Zone 3: the last entry, out to the stop loss. If price gets here, the move is more exhausted and the entry comes at a better price.
      • Take profit: where you take your profit.
      • Stop loss: where you close with a small loss if the plan fails.

      A simple way to use them is to split your stake into three parts, one per zone. If price turns at Zone 1 you profit with a third; if it sweeps down to Zone 3, you are fully in at a better price.

      Each entry (▲ Buy or ▼ Sell) is generated on its candle and stays fixed on the chart even if you reload the page: there is no repainting. The label shows whether it is meant as a CFD (trend entries and the oversold buy) or as a binary option (zone entry), with its expiry. While it is open it shows how many pips it is up or down, in green when in favour and in red when against; for the CFD, together with the stop loss and the take profit. The entries are the Umbral Entries indicator and can be hidden from Indicators.

      7. Trend and structure

      In an uptrend every high and every low is higher than the previous ones; in a downtrend, lower. When it does neither, the market is ranging.

      BOS
      Break of structure: price clears the last high (or low) in the direction of the trend. The trend continues.
      CHoCH
      Change of character: price breaks against the trend. It is the first warning of a possible reversal.
      Strong and Weak
      The high or low the market defends (strong) and the one it will probably break (weak).

      Dashed lines are the internal structure, a few candles long; solid lines, the larger structure.

      On every market, on the 30-minute, 1-hour and 4-hour charts, entries are generated in the direction of the underlying trend. Internally, without drawing it, Umbral follows a zigzag on a higher timeframe that defines the direction of that trend: 4-hour on the 30-minute chart and 1-day on the 1-hour and 4-hour charts. Within a downtrend, every upward correction ends at some point: when the chart confirms that turn, the sell entry is generated; in an uptrend, the buy entry. The direction of the trend is shown next to the chart name. Every position is managed with a trailing stop and no fixed take profit: the stop starts at the extreme of the correction, advances with each confirmed low (with each high, for sells) and the position closes when it is hit or when the trend changes.

      Each entry has a target set at entry: the previous high or low, the next 1-day or 4-hour zone against it or, if there is none, 2 times the risk. It does not close when the target is reached: the trade stays open as long as the trend lasts.

      To protect the gain, the stop moves to the entry (break-even) when price reaches the target and, on forex and indices, as soon as it advances one times the risk. From then on the trade can no longer end in a loss, and the trailing stop keeps advancing with each turn.

      On gold, silver (4 hours), Brent oil (4 hours), Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, XRP, BNB, Avalanche, Chainlink and Cardano the scale-ins are also generated (on the 30-minute chart, only on gold, Ethereum and Cardano): one more entry in favour when the trade is half a risk ahead (Scale-in 1) and another when it is one risk ahead (Scale-in 2). Each scale-in risks the same as the initial entry and, with each one, the stop of all of them moves up one step: with the first, to the entry; with the second, to half a risk in profit. From there they follow the trailing stop together and close at the same time. On Dogecoin, Litecoin and WTI oil they are not generated. On gold, forex and indices there is no scaling in once the underlying leg already measures 1.5 times the previous one: the move is close to its end.

      On the 1, 3, 5 and 15-minute charts entries are only taken with price outside the Bollinger band (20, 2), at least half a standard deviation beyond it. Before each entry the 1-hour, 4-hour and 1-day trend is checked: if all three point in the direction of the stretch, the entry goes with it, a CFD with the stop at the Bollinger average, which follows it candle by candle. Otherwise the shots go against the stretch, as a ladder: the first when price leaves the band, the second half a standard deviation further out and the third one more, on later candles. The product then depends on the chart's underlying trend, the 1-hour one on the 1 and 3-minute charts and the 4-hour one on the 5 and 15-minute charts. If the stretch goes against it (a pullback), the shots are CFDs with a common stop 2 standard deviations beyond the first; when price returns to the average, half is closed and the stop on the rest moves to the average entry price, with the target at the opposite band. If the stretch goes with that trend, only short binaries are shown, which at most lose the stake: they expire in 5 minutes on synthetics and gold on the 1 and 3-minute charts, and in 15 minutes elsewhere. On crypto, oil and natural gas, where Deriv offers no binaries, that shot is not taken. With no clear underlying trend, a CFD and a binary at the same time. It is a high-risk approach: if price keeps stretching, the stop takes all the shots.

      On EUR/USD, USD/CAD and EUR/GBP, zone entries are added. When price touches the midpoint of a 1-day or 4-hour support or resistance for the first time, with price overextended, entry 1 is generated; if the move continues, entry 2 at the midpoint of the next zone and entry 3 one level further. Each is shown as a binary option: Rise at a support and Fall at a resistance, expiring at the close of the 1-hour candle after the one of the touch. No entries are generated on later touches of the same zone.

      No entry is right every time: what matters is following the plan, limiting the risk of each trade and letting the ones that move in your favour run.

      On the daily chart of the US 500, the US Tech 100 and the Wall Street 30, the oversold buy is also generated: the index pulls back sharply (2-day RSI below 10) while holding above its 200-day average. The exit is the first close above the 5-day average or after 10 sessions, with a stop loss at 2 ATR. It is a mean-reversion strategy: gains tend to be small and a single loss can be twice their size. On all three indices the weak-close buy is also generated, when the session closes in the lowest 20% of its range. On the US Tech 100 it is shown as a CFD (buy at the close, stop loss at 2 ATR, exit on the first close above the previous session high or after 5 sessions) and as a Rise binary option over 7 days; on the US 500 and the Wall Street 30, only as a binary. The daily-chart buys are also marked on the 30-minute, 1-hour and 4-hour charts, on the candle where that day's session closes. On the US 500 there is a third buy, the pullback buy: above the 200-day average, three consecutive lower closes or the 2-day RSI of two sessions below 35, with the same exit as the oversold buy.

      On the daily chart of cryptocurrencies, gold, silver and oil the channel breakout is generated: a buy when the day closes above the high of the previous 20 days (on oil, also a sell below the low), with an initial stop at 3 ATR and a trailing stop 3 ATR from the best close, with no fixed target. It is one of the best-known trend-following systems; on forex and indices it is not generated.

      8. Liquidity, order blocks and volume

      Large traders need many orders on the other side to get in. Those orders gather where people place their stops: just above highs and just below lows. That is liquidity, and price usually goes for it before turning.

      • Liquidity (white dashed line): highs or lows price has not swept yet. ×2 or ×3 means several sit together.
      • EQH / EQL: equal highs or lows, very visible liquidity.
      • Order block: the last candle before a strong move, where large orders probably came in. Green if it pushed up, red if it pushed down.
      • Volume profile (grey bars on the right): where most trading happened. The POC (amber dotted line) is the price with the most volume.

      9. Indicators, in two sentences

      RSI
      Measures the strength of recent gains against losses, from 0 to 100. Above 70 price has risen a lot; below 30, it has fallen a lot.
      Stochastic
      Tells you whether price is near the top or the bottom of recent candles. Useful to spot turns in a zone.
      MACD
      Compares two price averages. When it crosses up, the rise gains strength; when it crosses down, the fall does.
      Moving averages
      The average price of the last candles (20, 50 or 200). If price is above the 200 average, the underlying trend is up.
      Bollinger
      A channel around the average. Touching the lower or upper edge means price has stretched.
      ADX
      Measures whether there is a trend, not its direction. Below 20, a ranging market; above 25, a clear trend.

      No indicator is right on its own. Umbral combines them and tells you how many agree and how many disagree.

      Level: the high, low and close of the previous day, week and month, the opens, Monday's high and low, and round numbers. Many traders place their orders there, so price often reacts when it gets there.

      Trend by timeframe: top right of the chart, from 30 minutes to 1 day. Green, bullish; red, bearish; light green or orange, it has just changed. Next to the chart's name you can see whether its timeframe is bullish or bearish and since when. Under the price, the countdown until the candle closes.

      10. Binaries

      You choose Up or Down, how much you stake and how long it lasts (from 15 seconds to 1 day). If, when time runs out, price is where you said, you get your stake back plus 90%; if not, you lose all of it. With “Allow equals”, you also win if it ends at the same price.

      On the chart you see the entry line, the countdown and a chequered flag where it expires. Binaries cannot be closed early.

      A binary option, also called a digital option, is a fixed-payout derivative: it pays a set amount if the condition is met at expiry and nothing if it is not. It did not originate with retail brokers: digital options have been traded between financial institutions for decades, and in the United States they are listed on CFTC-regulated exchanges.

      Their sale to retail clients has been prohibited in the European Union since 2018 (an ESMA decision, now carried into national rules such as the Spanish CNMV Resolution of 27 June 2019) and in the United Kingdom since April 2019 (FCA). Supervisors adopted the measure because their analyses found consistent losses on retail accounts. The prohibition does not extend to professional clients, who may trade them with authorised firms. Elsewhere it depends on each country's regulation.

      Umbral Trade does not sell or broker binary options or CFDs and does not act on behalf of any broker: it shows on the chart how each entry would be set up with either instrument. Before trading, check which products you are allowed to trade given your country of residence and your client category.

      11. CFDs and leverage

      With a CFD you choose Buy (you win if it rises) or Sell (you win if it falls), and you close it whenever you want. Leverage multiplies your stake: with €10 at 1:100 you control €1,000, and every 1% the price moves is €10 for or against you.

      When you open it you pay a small commission, a percentage of the position size, so the trade starts slightly negative. If the loss, including the commission, reaches what you invested, the trade closes on its own: that is the stop out, the orange line on the chart. You can set take profit and stop loss when opening or change them while the trade is open. The higher the leverage, the less price has to move for you to lose it all.

      12. Protecting your money

      • Do not risk more than 1 or 2% of your money on a single trade.
      • Decide your stop loss before entering, not after.
      • After a loss, do not double your stake to win it back.
      • Practise with the virtual balance until your results are steady for weeks.
      • Only trade money you can afford to lose. Binaries and CFDs are very high-risk products and most people who use them lose money.

      13. News

      Major economic releases, like US jobs or inflation data, move forex, indices, commodities and crypto. Near the release time, supports and resistances hold worse. On the chart they appear as small yellow flags: tap them to see the release and what it affects. Synthetics have no news.

      14. Glossary

      Leverage
      How many times your stake is multiplied in a CFD (1:100 = 100 times).
      ATR
      How much a candle moves on average. It measures distances according to how nervous each market is.
      Sweep
      When price briefly pierces a support or resistance, triggers the stops and comes back.
      Buy / Sell
      Win if price rises / win if it falls.
      Heikin Ashi
      Candles that average their price with the previous candle. They are smoother and show the trend better, but they do not show the exact price.
      Stake
      The money you put into a trade. It is the most you can lose on it.
      Ranging
      A market with no trend, moving from side to side.
      Wick
      The thin line of a candle: how far price reached and gave back.
      Pip
      The usual smallest step of a forex pair: 0.0001 on EUR/USD.
      Breakout
      When price breaks through a support or resistance and keeps going.
      Spread
      The difference between the buy and sell price: the cost of entering with a real broker.
      Stop loss
      The price at which you close with a small loss if you are wrong.
      Stop out
      The automatic close of a CFD when the loss equals the stake.
      Take profit
      The price at which you close in profit.
      Trend
      The underlying direction of price: up, down or ranging.
      Zone 1, 2 and 3
      The three entry bands of an Umbral plan, from the first price touches to the last.
