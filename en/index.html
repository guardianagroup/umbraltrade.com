      Three entry zones, never more than three

      Price rarely turns exactly at the support or resistance. That is why every support and every resistance comes with three staggered entry zones and a point where the plan stops being valid. Supports are green and resistances are red.

      At a support, price comes down

      We mark price turns on 1 hour, 4 hours and 1 day. A 1-day turn weighs three times a 1-hour one, and supports and resistances that price has already broken twice are discarded. Few remain, and they are the ones that matter.

      Zone 1

      Price touches the support or resistance. First entry, right at the edge. This is the alert you receive.

      Zone 2

      Price often pushes through the support or resistance to collect the liquidity behind it, then comes back. That is the second entry. The distance comes from that asset's real sweeps.

      Zone 3

      If the sweep runs deep there is a third entry, with a better average price. There is no fourth.

      Stop loss

      If price gets here, the support or resistance has broken and the plan is cancelled. You know it before you enter.

      At a resistance, upwards

      Same plan, upside down. Price rises: zone 1 is the first it touches, at the resistance itself. Zones 2 and 3 sit above it, and the stop at the very top. The number always follows the order in which price arrives.

      What weighs in each alert

      Supports and resistances read with Smart Money Concepts: structure, liquidity and sweeps. Our own version, measured asset by asset.

      The more turns and timeframes line up at a price, the stronger the support or resistance.
      Untouched liquidity

      Structure, discount and premium
      Upside or downside breaks on 1 hour, 4 hours and 1 day, and whether price sits in the cheap or expensive half of the range.

      Questions

      Where do the prices come from?

      In real time. Synthetics, forex, metals, indices, Bitcoin and Ethereum come from Deriv's public server: the same prices you see on its platform. The other cryptocurrencies come from Binance, and oil and natural gas from Hyperliquid, a contracts platform that tracks their price.

      Can I practise without risking money?

      Yes. Next to each asset's chart there is a panel to open binaries (Up or Down) and CFDs (Buy or Sell, with the leverage you choose) with €10,000 of virtual money. You see them drawn on the chart with their time left, and the Practice page keeps your history and your hit rate.

      Does it work for binaries and CFDs?

      Yes. The alert gives you the direction and the three entry prices. You choose the instrument, the size and the expiry.

      Do supports work on synthetic indices?

      Synthetics are generated by an audited random algorithm and do not react to news. The radar marks where price turned, measures how often those levels held on each index and shows it next to the chance benchmark, so you decide with data. No support guarantees a bounce.

      Is this financial advice?

      No. Umbral Trade is an analysis tool. It does not manage money or guarantee results. Trading with leverage can make you lose everything you invested.

      Practice mode

      Trade with virtual money on live prices, to learn to enter at the three zones without risking anything. Binaries: Up or Down, from 15 seconds to 1 day. CFDs: Buy or Sell with leverage from 1:100 to 1:1000, closed whenever you want.

      Practice balance

      Virtual money. Binaries always pay 90%. CFDs pay a commission when opened, half of what Deriv charges on that asset, and have no spread: with a real broker results may differ.

      1. What trading is

      Trading means opening positions to profit from price moves: buying if you think it will go up and selling if you think it will go down. You do not need to own the asset: with binaries and CFDs you only bet on the direction of the price.

      Nobody is always right. The difference between those who last and those who blow up their account is entering at good places and losing little when wrong.

      2. The markets you will see here

      Forex
      Currency pairs, like EUR/USD: how many dollars one euro is worth. Open Monday to Friday.
      Commodities
      Gold, silver, platinum, palladium, WTI oil, Brent oil and natural gas.
      Indices
      A basket of a country's large companies, like the US 500 or Germany 40.
      Crypto
      Bitcoin, Ethereum and other cryptocurrencies. They move a lot and never close.
      Synthetics
      Deriv indices that imitate a market. They trade 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and news does not affect them. Volatility 75 moves more than Volatility 10.

      3. Candles: how to read the chart

      Each candle sums up what price did over a period: where it opened, where it closed, and the highest and lowest it reached. Green: it closed higher than it opened, so it rose. Red: it closed lower, so it fell.

      The body runs from the open to the close. The wicks are where price reached and then gave back. A long lower wick at a support says buyers defended it.

      Heikin Ashi is another way to draw candles. Each one averages its price with the previous one, so they come out smoother: rises chain green candles and falls chain red ones, and the trend shows at a glance. Turn it on with the Heikin Ashi button on the chart bar (HA on small screens). Because they are an average, they do not show the exact price: when you trade, go by the current price label, which always shows the real price.

      4. Timeframes

      It is how long each candle lasts: 1 minute (1m), 5 minutes (5m), 15 minutes, 1 hour (1H), 4 hours (4H) or 1 day (1D). It is the same price seen with more or less zoom.

      A useful rule: find the level on a high timeframe (1H, 4H or 1D) and fine-tune the entry on a low one (1m or 5m). That is why the radar watches 1-hour, 4-hour and 1-day supports and resistances, and you can open up to four charts at once.

      5. Support and resistance

      A support is an area below the price where buyers showed up before and price bounced up. A resistance is an area above where sellers showed up and price fell.

      In Umbral a support is always green and below, and a resistance is always red and above. They are bands, not lines: price rarely turns at the exact cent. The higher the timeframe and the more times price has turned there, the stronger the zone.

      If price breaks a support and really moves away, that level can start acting as resistance. Umbral only changes its colour once price has moved far enough away.

      6. The three entry zones

      Price almost never turns right at the edge: it often goes a little further, sweeps the stops of those who entered early and then turns. That is why Umbral spreads the entry over three consecutive bands. Their width is not fixed: it comes from what price has done on that asset, so a jumpy asset has wider zones and a calm one narrower zones.

      • Zone 1: the first thing price touches, from the edge of the support or resistance to where price usually goes before turning. It has the strongest colour.
      • Zone 2: the sweep, where liquidity usually gets triggered, out to the deepest sweeps.
      • Zone 3: the last entry, out to the stop loss. If price gets here, the move is more exhausted and the entry comes at a better price.
      • Take profit: where you take your profit.
      • Stop loss: where you close with a small loss if the plan fails.

      A simple way to use them is to split your stake into three parts, one per zone. If price turns at Zone 1 you profit with a third; if it sweeps down to Zone 3, you are fully in at a better price.

      When price turns inside a zone, or after stretching far (outside the Nadaraya-Watson envelope and the Bollinger band, with RSI at an extreme), the chart marks an arrow on the very candle of the turn: ▲ Buy at a bottom and ▼ Sell at a top, at supports and at resistances alike. Only turns already confirmed by a closed candle are marked; once an arrow is marked it stays where it is, even if the zones change or you reload the page. The arrows are the Umbral Entries indicator: they stay when you remove Umbral Zones, and you turn them off from Indicators. When volatility is abnormal (a news release, a shock) they are not drawn. Next to the chart name you can see what share of those signals is right on that asset and timeframe: the algorithm checks them against what happened next and, while it has few, starts from what markets of the same family do.

      7. Trend and structure

      In an uptrend every high and every low is higher than the previous ones; in a downtrend, lower. When it does neither, the market is ranging.

      BOS
      Break of structure: price clears the last high (or low) in the direction of the trend. The trend continues.
      CHoCH
      Change of character: price breaks against the trend. It is the first warning of a possible reversal.
      Strong and Weak
      The high or low the market defends (strong) and the one it will probably break (weak).

      Dashed lines are the internal structure, a few candles long; solid lines, the larger structure.

      8. Liquidity, order blocks and volume

      Large traders need many orders on the other side to get in. Those orders gather where people place their stops: just above highs and just below lows. That is liquidity, and price usually goes for it before turning.

      • Liquidity (white dashed line): highs or lows price has not swept yet. ×2 or ×3 means several sit together.
      • EQH / EQL: equal highs or lows, very visible liquidity.
      • Order block: the last candle before a strong move, where large orders probably came in. Green if it pushed up, red if it pushed down.
      • Volume profile (grey bars on the right): where most trading happened. The POC (amber dotted line) is the price with the most volume.

      9. Indicators, in two sentences

      RSI
      Measures the strength of recent gains against losses, from 0 to 100. Above 70 price has risen a lot; below 30, it has fallen a lot.
      Stochastic
      Tells you whether price is near the top or the bottom of recent candles. Useful to spot turns in a zone.
      MACD
      Compares two price averages. When it crosses up, the rise gains strength; when it crosses down, the fall does.
      Moving averages
      The average price of the last candles (20, 50 or 200). If price is above the 200 average, the underlying trend is up.
      Bollinger
      A channel around the average. Touching the lower or upper edge means price has stretched.
      ADX
      Measures whether there is a trend, not its direction. Below 20, a ranging market; above 25, a clear trend.

      No indicator is right on its own. Umbral combines them and tells you how many agree and how many disagree.

      10. Binaries

      You choose Up or Down, how much you stake and how long it lasts (from 15 seconds to 1 day). If, when time runs out, price is where you said, you get your stake back plus 90%; if not, you lose all of it. With “Allow equals”, you also win if it ends at the same price.

      On the chart you see the entry line, the countdown and a chequered flag where it expires. Binaries cannot be closed early.

      11. CFDs and leverage

      With a CFD you choose Buy (you win if it rises) or Sell (you win if it falls), and you close it whenever you want. Leverage multiplies your stake: with €10 at 1:100 you control €1,000, and every 1% the price moves is €10 for or against you.

      When you open it you pay a small commission, a percentage of the position size, so the trade starts slightly negative. If the loss, including the commission, reaches what you invested, the trade closes on its own: that is the stop out, the orange line on the chart. You can set take profit and stop loss when opening or change them while the trade is open. The higher the leverage, the less price has to move for you to lose it all.

      12. Protecting your money

      • Do not risk more than 1 or 2% of your money on a single trade.
      • Decide your stop loss before entering, not after.
      • After a loss, do not double your stake to win it back.
      • Practise with the virtual balance until your results are steady for weeks.
      • Only trade money you can afford to lose. Binaries and CFDs are very high-risk products and most people who use them lose money.

      13. News

      Major economic releases, like US jobs or inflation data, move forex, indices, commodities and crypto. Near the release time, supports and resistances hold worse. On the chart they appear as small yellow flags: tap them to see the release and what it affects. Synthetics have no news.

      14. Glossary

      Leverage
      How many times your stake is multiplied in a CFD (1:100 = 100 times).
      ATR
      How much a candle moves on average. It measures distances according to how nervous each market is.
      Sweep
      When price briefly pierces a support or resistance, triggers the stops and comes back.
      Buy / Sell
      Win if price rises / win if it falls.
      Heikin Ashi
      Candles that average their price with the previous candle. They are smoother and show the trend better, but they do not show the exact price.
      Stake
      The money you put into a trade. It is the most you can lose on it.
      Ranging
      A market with no trend, moving from side to side.
      Wick
      The thin line of a candle: how far price reached and gave back.
      Pip
      The usual smallest step of a forex pair: 0.0001 on EUR/USD.
      Breakout
      When price breaks through a support or resistance and keeps going.
      Spread
      The difference between the buy and sell price: the cost of entering with a real broker.
      Stop loss
      The price at which you close with a small loss if you are wrong.
      Stop out
      The automatic close of a CFD when the loss equals the stake.
      Take profit
      The price at which you close in profit.
      Trend
      The underlying direction of price: up, down or ranging.
      Zone 1, 2 and 3
      The three entry bands of an Umbral plan, from the first price touches to the last.
